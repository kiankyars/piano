# First seven sessions

Open the [practice page](https://kiankyars.github.io/piano/) and follow the hour. Session 1 uses the placement check instead of the usual reading block. This shortlist removes the first few days of searching; it is not a rigid difficulty ladder.

Use the first **4–8 bars** of each suggested score initially. You do not need to finish an entire piece. If a selection introduces too many unfamiliar notes or rhythms, use the next suitable standard-staff score in [First Pieces](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebiesFP.html) or [Primer A](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebiesP.html). Skip entries labelled “no staffs” and choose plain notation rather than color-coded/letter-name versions.

| Session | Focus | First score to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Placement: choose a tempo that allows continuity | [Les Cloches](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pLesCloches/pLesCloches.pdf) |
| 2 | Bass clef and the next hand entry | [Goodnight, Ladies](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pGoodnightLadies/pGoodnightLadies.html) |
| 3 | Count through rests | [Five Fat Turkeys Are We](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pFiveFatTurkeys/pFiveFatTurkeys.html) |
| 4 | Notice repeated directions and intervals | [The Juniper Tree](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pJuniperTree/pJuniperTree.html) |
| 5 | Coordinate the hands without losing the beat | [Yonder She Comes](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pYonderSheComes/pYonderSheComes.html) |
| 6 | Add one expressive choice on easy music | [Gray Squirrel](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/Primer/pGraySquirrel/pGraySquirrel.html) |
| 7 | Record an unseen benchmark at comparable difficulty | Choose an unplayed, unfamiliar title from the same level; reserve it until today |

These focus prompts are tasks for you, not claims that a score uniquely teaches that feature. For additional excerpts during the fifteen-minute reading block, use other unread scores at the same level. Leave the seven suggested first scores untouched until their session. If you know a tune well enough to play by ear, it is still useful reading practice but a weaker benchmark.

## The continuing queue

At the end of each session, note the next unread title. On the next day:

1. Open your current library: [First Pieces](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebiesFP.html), [Primer](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebiesP.html), [Level 1](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebies1.html) or [Level 2](https://www.gmajormusictheory.org/Freebies/freebies2.html).
2. Work through titles in page order, skipping unfamiliar technical demands that exceed your current stage. The library's ordering is broad, not an exact sight-reading progression.
3. Record title and bars already read. A different excerpt of a long piece can supply fresh notation, but repeated musical patterns make it less useful as an independent benchmark.
4. If you exhaust suitable scores, use a graded book or the optional exercise service in [resources](resources.md). Do not move to overly hard music simply because you finished a list.

No need to learn all of these pieces for performance. The purpose is many manageable first encounters with notation.
