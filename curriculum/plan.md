# A practical sight-reading plan

## The daily hour

Spend **30 minutes reading exercises on the [practice page](https://kiankyars.github.io/piano/)**, then **30 minutes on your pieces**. Stay on the page for reading: it supplies the score and the next exercise.

Keep K545 and Rondo alla Turca in the pieces half. Their memorized difficulty does not establish your reading level. Reading unfamiliar, much easier music gives you a different task: turn notation into musical patterns and keyboard movement while keeping time.

The exercises are original four-bar patterns with constrained notes, rhythms and textures. They provide convenient first-reading practice, not a complete piano method or a promise of a particular grade. The progression below is a practical starting point that you can adjust from what happens at the keyboard.

## First session: find your starting point

Open level 1. Both staves are conventional five-line notation: treble for the right hand, bass for the left. There are no printed finger numbers or note names. The right-hand notes stay within C4–G4 and the left-hand notes within C3–G3; C4 is middle C. Find the starting notes before playing.

Choose a slow pulse, preview the four bars, then play once without restarting. If you repeatedly stop, read one hand or just the first two bars, then try the complete exercise. If the full exercise is comfortable, choose **Next exercise** and confirm that on another example. Sample the next level when the current demand is clearly easy; do not stay at a level just to fill a calendar week.

In 4/4 or 3/4, quarter note = 40–60 BPM is a possible starting point, not a requirement. Choose a speed that works through the hardest part. If even a very slow pulse is repeatedly interrupted, simplify the task instead of stretching each beat indefinitely. Playback tempo is a reference, not a target you must match.

## The reading half-hour

**Preview.** Check the key signature, meter, first notes, rests and repeated shapes. Locate your hands and count a bar in. Choose comfortable fingers; the task is to read the notes and their movement, not decode printed finger numbers. Do not listen to playback or rehearse the whole exercise before the first attempt.

**Play once.** Keep a gentle, continuous pulse. Let a wrong note go and recover at the next beat. Read a little ahead when possible. Avoid pedal initially so that rests, durations and coordination remain audible. Use brief keyboard glances when you need them.

**Check, then continue.** Say whether you kept the pulse and what caused the main hesitation. Use **Hear it** after your attempt if a sound or rhythm is unclear, and compare it with the notation. Repair one short problem, then choose **Next exercise**. Repeating a passage helps you learn it; the next generated exercise gives you another first-reading opportunity.

If a specific problem keeps blocking the music, briefly isolate it on the same score. Find the uncertain bass note, tap the rhythm, or try the two hands separately, then return to the exercise. There are no separate daily drill slots.

## Six levels

These levels describe this generator's demands, **not ABRSM or RCM grades**. All exercises are four bars on a standard grand staff. There is no deadline for advancing.

| Level | What changes | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | C major five-note positions; hands alternate | Starting notes, steps, repeated notes and rests |
| 2 | C major melody over held bass notes | Keep one hand sounding while the other moves |
| 3 | C, G and F major; some eighth notes | Key signature, stepwise direction and subdivisions |
| 4 | Simple left-hand two-note chords or broken patterns | Recognize a shape and keep the melody moving |
| 5 | 3/4 phrases in familiar keys | Three quarter-note beats and the next bar's arrival |
| 6 | Gentle 6/8 in familiar keys | Two dotted-quarter beats, each split into three eighths |

The page's level selector changes the exercise; it does not advance automatically. If 6/8 is new, tap two groups of three subdivisions before playing. At the other levels, count the meter shown rather than assuming every bar contains four quarter-note beats.

## When to advance or simplify

A useful heuristic is **three fresh exercises on different days** with a continuous pulse, no restarts and roughly 90% correct pitches and durations. Include both hands when the level calls for them. This is a self-check, not an official pass mark. If unsure about accuracy, say so rather than inventing a percentage.

Keep the tempo and conditions comparable when judging progress. Comfortable repetitions of a familiar exercise do not show the same thing as a fluent first attempt. When a key signature, chord shape or new meter causes repeated stops, stay at that demand or return to an easier level. There is no need to increase speed every time you change levels.

Occasionally compare fresh exercises at a familiar level and tempo. Look for fewer stops, quicker recognition and more attention left for the sound. If progress stalls, check task difficulty and practice consistency; a teacher can hear and see problems this page cannot assess.

## The pieces half-hour

Spend **30 minutes on your pieces**. Improve one useful passage or learn part of a new, easy piece from notation, then enjoy playing. Settle fingering slowly, reconnect the passage to its surrounding phrase, and listen for a clear musical goal such as an even accompaniment or a distinct melody.

Alternate familiar Mozart with easier new music. Isolate a troublesome movement briefly when useful; technique does not need a separate timed block. Play comfortably, stop if playing hurts, and seek hands-on feedback for recurring tension.

## Keeping this useful over time

Attach practice to an existing daily cue. Open the page and begin with the exercise already there. On a busy day, shorten the same two parts instead of abandoning the session; missing a day creates no catch-up debt.

The generated exercises deliberately cover a narrow range of reading situations. As they become comfortable, include longer composed pieces, unfamiliar textures, articulation and phrase shaping within your reading time. The [optional further-study resources](resources.md) offer ways to broaden that work when useful. They are not needed to complete today's practice.

No plan can promise the fastest progress or a particular reading level after a fixed number of hours. The aim is a change in what you can read the first time, while keeping playing enjoyable enough to continue.
