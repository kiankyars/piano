Piano practice

30 minutes of sight-reading. 30 minutes of pieces.

1. Sight-reading — 30 minutes

Open scores: First Pieces → Primer

Pick a short excerpt you haven’t played. Look it over, choose a slow beat, and play through without restarting. Check the difficult spot afterward, then move to fresh music.

Start with 4–8 bars. If you keep stopping, choose easier music or try one hand.

Start with Les Cloches. There are more suggestions in the starting score list.

2. Pieces — 30 minutes

Work on a piece you want to play. Choose one passage or problem, practice it slowly, then put it back into the phrase. Keep time to play for enjoyment. Alternate your Mozart pieces with easier new music learned from the score.

If you get stuck

Use a drill only for the problem you’re having, then return to the score.

  • Note reading: choose the clef and range you need; play each note on the piano.
  • Rhythm: count and tap before checking playback.

For an awkward movement, practice that small pattern comfortably and slowly.

About this reading level

Read small patterns in both clefs, and keep a slow pulse.

Start in C major with steps, repeated notes and thirds.

Quarter, half and whole notes in 4/4.

When to advance: Try three unfamiliar excerpts on different days. Advance when you can keep the pulse with roughly 90% of pitches and durations correct, without restarting.

The stage numbers are this curriculum’s sequence, not exam grades. There is no deadline to advance.
